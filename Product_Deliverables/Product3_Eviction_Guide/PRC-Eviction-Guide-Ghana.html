Chapter 1

What the Law Says

Act 220, Rent Control, and why self-help eviction is illegal

Most landlords in Ghana believe they have the right to evict a tenant whenever they wish. This belief has led to serious legal consequences for landlords who take matters into their own hands.

The Rent Act 1963 (Act 220)

The Rent Act 1963, also called Act 220, is the primary law that governs landlord-tenant relationships in Ghana. This law was written to protect both landlords and tenants, but it is often misunderstood.

Under Act 220, a landlord cannot simply remove a tenant, change the locks, remove the roof, cut utilities, or use threats to force a tenant to leave. All of these actions are illegal and can result in criminal prosecution of the landlord.

⚠ Warning

Self-help eviction is a criminal offense. Landlords who change locks, cut utilities, remove roofing, or threaten tenants can be prosecuted and imprisoned.

What Is Legal Eviction?

Legal eviction means following a strict process laid out in the Rent Act 1963. This process includes serving formal notices, filing an application with the Rent Control Department, attending a hearing, and obtaining a court order.

Why the Law Requires This Process

The reason the law requires this process is to ensure fairness. A tenant has the right to:

  • Know in writing why the landlord wants them to leave
  • Have time to respond to the notice
  • Be heard by a neutral official (the Rent Magistrate)
  • Challenge false accusations
  • Present evidence in their own defense

When you follow the legal process, you protect yourself and the tenant. You also create a paper trail that proves you acted lawfully if the tenant later makes false claims.

✓ Key Takeaway

Legal eviction takes time and follows steps set by law. The investment in following the proper process protects you from criminal charges and ensures the court will enforce your right to recover possession.

Chapter 2

The 6 Lawful Grounds for Recovery of Possession

What the law permits, and what it does not

Under the Rent Act 1963, a landlord cannot evict a tenant for any reason. The law lists specific grounds that are lawful. If your reason for eviction is not one of these grounds, the Rent Magistrate will dismiss your case.

The 6 Lawful Grounds

1

Non-Payment of Rent

The tenant has failed to pay rent for one or more months. This is the most common ground for eviction.

2

Breach of Tenancy Agreement

The tenant has broken an important term of the agreement. Examples: subletting, running a business, keeping prohibited animals, or causing damage.

3

Nuisance or Annoyance

The tenant is causing a nuisance to neighbors or affecting others' use and enjoyment of the property.

4

Deterioration of the Property

The tenant has allowed the property to fall into disrepair or has caused damage beyond normal wear and tear.

5

Landlord's Own Use

The landlord intends to occupy the property for their own use or that of a family member.

6

End of Fixed-Term Agreement

The fixed-term tenancy agreement has expired, and the landlord does not wish to renew it.

What Is NOT a Lawful Ground

The following reasons do NOT permit eviction under the Rent Act:

  • The landlord simply wants the tenant out for any reason
  • Personal dislike of the tenant
  • The property will be sold (the new owner must honor the tenancy)

Important Note

If you evict a tenant on an unlawful ground, the Rent Magistrate will reject your application. Before filing, verify that your ground is one of the six lawful grounds.

Glossary

Act 220
The Rent Act 1963 of Ghana, the primary law governing landlord-tenant relationships.
Arrears
Rent or other payments that are overdue and unpaid.
Bailiff
An officer of the court authorized to enforce court orders, including eviction enforcement.
Breach
A violation of a term of the tenancy agreement or a failure to perform an obligation under the law.
Counter-Application
A formal response filed by a tenant in response to a landlord's eviction application.
Cure (or Remedy)
To fix or stop a breach; for example, paying overdue rent or stopping nuisance behavior.
Execution (or Enforcement)
The process of enforcing a court order, typically through a bailiff removing the tenant.
Form 9
The official application for recovery of possession of a residential property under the Rent Act 1963.
Judgment
The decision of the Rent Magistrate on whether to grant or refuse an application for eviction.
Quit Notice
A formal written notice requiring the tenant to vacate the property within a specified period.
Rent Control Department
The government office in each region responsible for administering the Rent Act 1963.
Rent Magistrate
A judicial officer appointed under the Rent Act 1963 to hear and decide eviction cases.
Tenancy Agreement
A written or oral contract between a landlord and tenant setting out the terms of the rental.
Warning (or Notice to Cure)
A formal written notice giving the tenant an opportunity to fix a breach before eviction proceedings.
