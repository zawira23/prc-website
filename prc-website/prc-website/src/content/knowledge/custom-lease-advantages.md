## The Template Problem

You downloaded a lease template online or got one from a colleague. It's 10 pages long, looks professional, covers most situations. You fill in the tenant name and rent amount and use it.

But your situation isn't generic:
- Your property is furnished and the template doesn't cover inventory
- You're renting commercial space with special requirements
- The property has unique features (shared services, utilities included)
- The tenant is someone special (employee, family member)
- Local conditions in your area need to be addressed

When disputes arise, that generic lease doesn't protect you because it wasn't written for your situation.

## Why Templates Fall Short

**Too generic:**
- Language covers average situations, not yours
- Missing provisions unique to your property type
- Doesn't address local market or conditions

**Too vague:**
- Uses standard language that doesn't specify your situation
- Leaves room for interpretation and disputes
- Doesn't clarify who's responsible for what

**Too risky:**
- May include language that violates Act 220
- Might not include mandatory provisions
- Could expose you to tenant counter-claims

**Too cautious:**
- May not include clauses that protect landlords
- Might skip important provisions your situation needs
- Could leave gaps in your legal protection

## What a Custom Lease Protects

A lease drafted for your specific situation includes:

**Your property type:**
- Furnished vs. unfurnished-specific language
- Commercial vs. residential provisions
- Service agreements if you provide utilities
- Equipment inventory for furnished properties

**Your situation:**
- Unique property features
- Special tenant circumstances
- Local market conditions
- Specific responsibilities

**Your protection:**
- Clauses that protect your interests
- Clear termination procedures
- Unambiguous maintenance responsibilities
- Strong dispute resolution language

**Act 220 compliance:**
- All mandatory provisions
- Enforceable language
- Proper notice procedures
- Legally defensible terms

## Real Examples

**Template lease for furnished apartment:**
- Generic "tenant will not damage property"
- No inventory of what's included
- No condition documentation process
- Vague maintenance responsibilities

**Custom lease for furnished apartment:**
- Detailed inventory with photos
- Move-in/move-out condition checklist
- Clear responsibility for damage vs. normal wear
- Specific procedures for damage claims

**Template for commercial space:**
- Generic residential language
- No provision for business-specific needs
- Missing clauses for commercial utilities
- No provision for signage or alterations

**Custom for commercial space:**
- Business-specific language
- Clear utility cost allocation
- Signage and alteration provisions
- Commercial-appropriate termination language
