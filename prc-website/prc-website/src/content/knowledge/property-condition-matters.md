## The Dispute You Can't Win Without Documentation

Your tenant moves out and claims the property is in perfect condition. You want to claim damage costs against the deposit or increase rent based on deterioration. But you have no proof of the property's actual condition.

You remember what it looked like when the tenant moved in, but can't prove it. The tenant's memory is different. You end up in dispute with no evidence to back either position.

A landlord in the next building faced the same situation—but they had professional photos and a condition report from day one. They won the dispute in minutes.

## What Property Inspection Does

A professional inspection documents:
- Exact condition of every room and surface
- Photos of each area (20–40 high-quality images)
- Notes on any damage, deterioration, or repairs needed
- Estimates for repairs and maintenance
- A timeline and professional summary

When disputes arise, you have proof, not memories.

## Why This Matters in Disputes

**Deposit disputes:**
- Tenant claims all damage was pre-existing
- Landlord claims tenant caused damage
- Without inspection report: landlord's word vs. tenant's word
- With inspection report: clear photos and professional documentation

**Rent level disputes:**
- Landlord claims property justifies current rent
- Tenant claims property is deteriorating
- Without inspection: subjective arguments
- With inspection: documented condition supports rent position

**Insurance claims:**
- Damage occurs and you file a claim
- Insurer wants proof of pre-existing condition
- Without inspection: you're guessing at pre-damage condition
- With inspection: clear evidence of condition before damage

**Eviction cases:**
- You claim property damage as reason to evict
- Tenant claims no such damage occurred
- Without inspection: your word
- With inspection: photographic evidence

## Real Examples

**Scenario 1: Deposit Dispute**
- Tenant moves out and disputes deposit deduction for damage
- Without inspection report: You remember the walls being clean. Tenant remembers painting them. Dispute is unresolvable.
- With inspection report: Photos show exact wall condition on move-in. Tenant's claim of fresh paint is clearly false.

**Scenario 2: Rent Level**
- Tenant argues rent is too high because property is deteriorating
- Without inspection: You argue it's in good condition, tenant disagrees
- With inspection: Professional report documents condition, supports rent level

**Scenario 3: Insurance Claim**
- Burst pipe damages ceiling and walls
- Without inspection: You claim pre-pipe damage was minimal. Insurer has no way to verify.
- With inspection: Clear documentation of pre-damage condition. Insurance claim is straightforward.

## When to Get Inspections

**Move-in inspection:**
- First day tenant moves in, professional inspection
- Creates baseline for dispute prevention
- Protects you if tenant claims pre-existing damage later

**Annual inspection:**
- Check on property condition while tenant is occupying
- Document any deterioration
- Plan maintenance and repairs
- Track property condition over time

**Move-out inspection:**
- When tenant leaves, professional inspection
- Compare to move-in condition
- Document any damage caused by tenant
- Support deposit deduction decisions

**Before disputes:**
- Tenant is claiming damage you dispute
- Get inspection done immediately
- Professional documentation settles the dispute

## What Good Inspection Reports Include

- Room-by-room condition assessment
- 20–40 professional photographs
- Written notes on condition, damage, repairs needed
- Cost estimates for repairs
- Professional summary and timeline
- Legally usable as evidence
